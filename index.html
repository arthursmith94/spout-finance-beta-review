Spout Finance: what a “0% interest” loan actually costs

A hands-on review of the Spout beta paired with a docs-based economic teardown — where the design is genuinely novel, where the numbers don’t reconcile, and one bug that gets the headline number exactly backwards.

By Arthur Smith · 11 September 2026 · ~14 min read · Independent & unaffiliated
Tokenized equities Solana devnet Covered-call lending DeFi credit First public teardown

Spout Finance lets you buy tokenized US stocks on Solana and borrow stablecoins against them at 0% interest. It is one of the more interesting things I’ve seen in the tokenized-equity space this year, because the 0% is not a marketing gimmick funded by a token emission — it is funded by a real cash flow: weekly covered calls written on the collateral. That makes it worth taking seriously, and taking seriously means asking the question the docs never quite answer: if the loan is free, who is paying for it?

This review combines two passes. The first is a mechanism-and-economics teardown built from the public documentation (~40 /docs pages, cited inline). The second is a hands-on session against the live beta (beta.spout.finance) on 11 September 2026, running on Solana devnet. I could exercise every read-only surface and the full quote/validation logic; I could not broadcast an on-chain buy or borrow, because devnet funding is external and rate-limited (see Methodology). Findings that require a funded position are labelled Blocked.

01Executive summary

The strongest findings, in priority order. Severities are the reviewer’s.

“0% interest” is a cash-flow statement, not an economic one

Thesis

The borrower pays in capped upside, not cash. Priced as the option it is, the expected cost runs ~6–10% of the loan per year for low-vol collateral (AAPL/XOM-type) and 30–80% for the high-vol names the marketing targets (NVDA/MSTR/SMCI). The cost is real, state-contingent, and structurally understated by the docs’ “0.5% annualized assignment cost” figure.

The live app shows “Borrow Cost” of 0.00%/yr for exactly the three highest-volatility assets

Bug · Medium

NVDA, SMCI and BSOL all display 0.00%/yr while calmer names (GS 0.86%, XOM 0.80%) show real numbers. This is backwards: borrow cost here is funded by option premium, which rises with volatility. The single most decision-relevant number on the trade screen is wrong for the assets where it matters most.

The docs contradict themselves on who absorbs assignment losses

Design · High

Three pages say the borrower absorbs strike-vs-market; three others say the lender pool nets premium minus assignment cost with an insurance fund behind it. These describe two different products with opposite lender risk profiles. Everything downstream — “delta-neutral” lenders, fund sizing, the yield claims — depends on which is true.

Assignment during a borrow is a forced deleveraging and a taxable sale

Design · High

When calls are assigned, proceeds first repay the loan and the shares are sold at the strike. A max-LTV borrower can wake up with roughly half the shares, no loan, and a realized sale of the entire position — breaking the “no selling, no taxable event” promise (/docs/introduction) for the exact buy-and-hold persona the copy courts.

Flat 50% LTV + one premium pool = a cross-subsidy that rewards the wrong behaviour

Design · Medium

A GLD borrower’s cheap loan is paid for by an NVDA borrower’s capped upside. And a borrower at 10% LTV pays the same collateral-level cost as one at 50%, so small loans are up to 5× more expensive per dollar. The rational move is to max out LTV — the opposite of what the risk docs advise.

The one control for the headline feature couldn’t be actuated

Bug · Medium

The Leverage slider — the entire borrow flow — did not respond to mouse-drag or keyboard, so borrowed amount, borrower cost and health factor never populated. This is partly gated on a funded balance and needs re-testing, but a headline control that resists both pointer and keyboard is a real robustness/accessibility concern.

The through-line: Spout’s structure is coherent and genuinely novel — nobody else funds a 0% stablecoin loan from a covered-call program — but its disclosure and one live data feed currently mislead the user about the one thing that matters, price. The good news is that almost everything below is fixable with copy and a per-asset cost table rather than a redesign.

02What Spout is

Spout tokenizes US stocks and ETFs as spAssets — 1:1 claims on shares custodied at a US broker-dealer (the app names Alpaca Securities and shows on-chain proof-of-reserves) — and issues them on Solana. You lock spAssets as collateral and draw stablecoins up to 50% LTV at 0% interest. While your collateral is locked, Spout writes weekly out-of-the-money covered calls on the pooled shares at the broker; the premium (net of a 20% protocol fee that also seeds an insurance fund) is paid to a separate class of stablecoin lenders, split across a Senior tranche (~9% target) and a Junior tranche (~32% target). If a stock rallies through the strike, the shares are sold at the strike, your debt is repaid from the proceeds, and an optional Auto-Roll rebuys the position. That covered-call cash flow is what makes “0%” a real number rather than a subsidy — and also what the borrower is implicitly paying.

03The hands-on walkthrough

Live beta, Solana devnet, 11 Sep 2026. Wallet connected via Privy + Wallet-Standard + SIWS; zero console errors across the whole session.

Onboarding is three gates deep before you reach the product: a testnet email-and-passcode gate, then a vanity “Spout card” step (type a name, receive a numbered access card with Download / Copy / Post-on-X), then Privy wallet connect. The card is pure gamification — a click-wall in front of the app Friction · Low. Worth noting the gate accepts any email with the passcode: the copy says “the email…we sent you” but no verification is enforced.

Past the gate, the connect flow is the strongest part of the app: standards-based (Privy, Wallet-Standard, a SIWS sign-in message), connected first try, instant balance and account chips, clean disconnect. Two transparency touches the docs themselves lack show up in every trade panel footer: “Held 1:1 at Alpaca Securities · Reserves 100.2%” and an on-chain proof-of-reserves line. This is exactly the disclosure the docs teardown flagged as missing — good to see it surfaced in-product.

The buy quote is genuinely well built: live price, a USD-or-shares denomination toggle, correct fractional-share math ($10 → 0.05 NVDA), and a clear breakdown of amount, shares, estimated borrower cost, and cost today.

Bug reports

BUG-1 — “Borrow Cost” is 0.00%/yr for the three highest-volatility assets Medium

On the trade list, NVDA, SMCI and BSOL all show 0.00%/yr, while lower-vol names carry real figures (GS 0.86%, XOM 0.80%, GOOG 0.54%, AAPL 0.37%, GLD 0.06%). Borrow cost on Spout is funded by the covered-call giveaway, which grows with volatility — so NVDA and SMCI should be the most expensive names to borrow against, not free. This looks like missing options/vol data defaulting to zero. It is directly misleading to a borrower comparing assets, and it contradicts Spout’s own economics.

Repro: pass gate → connect wallet → read the Borrow Cost column. Reproduced on every run.

BUG-2 — The Leverage slider could not be actuated; the borrow quote stays inert Medium

The Leverage control (1.0×→2.0×, where 2.0× means “borrow half at 0%”) is a role="slider" handle. It could not be moved by mouse-drag or by keyboard (focus + End/ArrowRight); it stayed pinned at 1.0× and “Amount / Stocks borrowed” stayed $0.00 / 0. As a result the borrowed amount, estimated borrower cost, and any health-factor or liquidation figure were never reachable in the UI. This may be partly gated on a funded balance and should be re-verified with funds and a real pointer — but a headline feature whose only control resists both mouse and keyboard is a genuine robustness/accessibility concern.

Repro: connect → select NVDA → Amount $1,000 → attempt to drag or keyboard the Leverage handle.

BUG-3 — Buy amount has no balance or upper-bound validation in the quote Low–Med

With a $0 balance, entering 999999999 in the NVDA field happily quotes “Total…$999,999,999.00 / 4,579,383.61 shares” and “Your cost today $999,999,999.00” with no inline insufficient-balance or max warning — only the CTA quietly flipping to “Add Money.” A negative value (-5) is silently coerced to 5. Non-numeric input correctly yields no quote.

Repro: connect → NVDA → type a very large or negative amount.

BLOCKED — anything requiring a funded position Blocked

Health-factor boundary behaviour, the 50.00% vs 50.01% LTV cap, partial repay, deposit-while-cycle-open, liquidation preview, an actual spAsset mint, and wallet-switch KYC follow-through all require an open, funded position. Because the leverage quote never populated (BUG-2) and devnet funding was rate-limited, none of these were reachable. This is the single biggest coverage gap in the review.

The leverage tooltip gets it right — so why doesn’t the price?

Credit where due: the Leverage tooltip is honest and clear — “At 1.0× you pay full price. At 2.0× you put up half and borrow half at 0% interest. Higher leverage means more upside but also more borrower cost.” That last clause is the whole review in one sentence. The problem is that the number the app then shows for “more borrower cost” is $0.00 and 0.00%/yr (Figs. 3–4). The concept is disclosed; the quantity is not.

Friction points

Testnet funding is external and hard Friction · Med: the wallet menu points you to two rate-limited, captcha-gated faucets (Circle Test-USDC and a devnet SOL airdrop) with “paste the address above.” There is no one-click in-app devnet faucet. For a beta whose entire point is to get people to try buy/borrow, this stops most testers at the door — and it is why the on-chain path is Blocked above.

Discoverability of borrowing is poor Friction · Low: the “Borrow” nav tab isn’t a borrow flow — it’s a marketing splash (“Every $10,000 of eligible stock lets you borrow up to $5,000 USDC…Explore Stocks”) that routes back to Trade. Borrowing actually lives inside the Leverage slider on the Buy panel. A user who wants to borrow against existing holdings has no obvious path.

What isn’t live yet, and what works

The Earn side — the entire Senior/Junior lending product — is not live: the tab reads “Earn is coming soon. Lending vaults are on the way.” So none of the tranche economics could be exercised, and the lender-side half of every doc contradiction below remains untested in-app. I also encountered no KYC step anywhere reachable — no ID or passport request through the full gate → connect → buy → portfolio path — despite docs claiming “wallet-level KYC on all tokenized asset holders.” On devnet that’s effectively mock/auto, or deferred to on-chain settlement I couldn’t reach.

What works well is worth stating plainly: wallet connect is clean and standards-based; the buy quote is one of the better ones I’ve used in tokenized-equity apps; the Portfolio page has thoughtful empty states and a real overview chart; and the “Ask Spout” assistant (“Answers from our docs and FAQ”) ships with exactly the right canned prompt — “What does this actually cost me?” If that assistant answers the question honestly and quantitatively, it would do more for user trust than any amount of “0%” banner copy.

04The economics: is 0% really 0%?

Where the review gets quantitative. All figures are docs-based Black-Scholes estimates; treat them as order-of-magnitude, not audited.

Premium and assignment are the same option. The lender receives the call premium P; the borrower gives up the expected payoff of that same call, E[(Sₜ − K)⁺]. The only wedge between the two is the variance risk premium (VRP — the gap between implied and realized volatility that option sellers earn). Everything else is a fair-value transfer from borrower to lender. So the borrower’s expected cost ≈ P minus whatever VRP the borrower happens to capture — which, for single stocks, is small (Carr & Wu, 2009: single-name VRP is far thinner than index VRP). In plain terms: the borrower pays very close to the full premium the lender collects, just in capped upside instead of cash.

The empirical anchor is the CBOE BXM buy-write index: 1986–2012 it collected ~1.8%/month in premium yet delivered roughly the same total return as the S&P 500 with about two-thirds the volatility. Over 25 years the premium was almost entirely “paid back” through forgone upside. A Spout borrower is, economically, a QYLD/JEPI holder who also received a 0% loan — a framing that is both more honest and still compelling.

Two things fall out. First, the pool’s yield will be dominated by the high-vol names, and under the flat 50% LTV their borrowers cross-subsidise everyone else — a GLD borrower cannot generate enough premium to fund the advertised lender yield at any sane strike, so someone else is. Second, and more pointed: the persona the copy explicitly targets — the “long-term bull who wants leverage” on NVDA/MSTR — is the worst-fit user. A 2023-style NVDA year (+240%) means repeated assignment and a shrinking share count. Selling weekly calls against a high-conviction position is the anti-thesis of holding it.

The “0.5% annualized assignment cost” can’t coexist with “double-digit lender APY”

If borrowers really gave up only 0.5%/yr while lenders earned ≥7.8%/yr of collateral in premium from the same options, options would be priced at ~15× their expected payoff. Real implied/realized ratios are ~1.1–1.3. The 0.5% figure can only be a net lender-side loss statistic, or measured on strikes so far out that they can’t fund the advertised yield — either way it is not the borrower’s expected cost, and it should not sit in the borrower FAQ.

Read fairly, this is not a knock-out. Spout genuinely wins for three groups: users with no access to cheap margin (non-US retail — the emerging-market row is the real wedge); income investors who would sell covered calls on AAPL/XOM anyway; and anyone who values that the cost never accrues as cash. There are no funding-based margin calls, and the cost only lands in states of the world where the borrower is already richer (the stock rallied). That last property is a real, under-marketed feature. Where it loses is precisely the high-conviction, high-vol holder the marketing chases, the low-LTV borrower, and the US taxpayer with embedded gains.

The doc contradictions that need resolving

The economics above assume one reading of the mechanism. The docs support two, and never pick. These are quoted, not paraphrased:

  • Who absorbs assignment High — /docs/options-assignment: “the proceeds first cover any outstanding debt…the remainder is yours” and the FAQ “you absorb the difference between the strike and the market price” (borrower bears it) vs /docs/settlement-flow and /docs/insurance-fund: “premium received minus the cost of assignment…the fund is drawn when a cycle produces a net loss” (lender pool bears it). If the borrower bears it, lenders are pure premium receivers and the “delta-neutral” framing holds; if the pool bears it, lenders are short single-stock upside and the insurance fund (target ~2% of pool) is undersized by an order of magnitude against a jump day like 9 Apr 2025 (NVDA +19% in a session).
  • No taxable event High — /docs/introduction promises “no selling, no taxable event,” but assignment is a sale of the shares. The fix (a cash-settled close that buys back the ITM call so shares never leave the account) is the only version that keeps this promise for US persons.
  • Contradictions on fees and lockups Low — origination fee (“the only cost” vs “no origination fee”), withdrawal fee (“0%” vs “20 bps”), lockups (“no lockups” vs Junior’s “45-day notice”), and KYC (“wallet-level KYC on all holders” vs Path-B “no KYC required”) each appear both ways across pages. Individually minor; collectively they erode trust in the numbers.
  • Unnamed everything Med — the auditor (“the protocol has been audited,” no firm, no report), the legal wrapper of spAssets, and the money-market venue for idle capital are all unstated. The app naming Alpaca and showing proof-of-reserves is a good precedent to extend.

05Recommendations, prioritised

  1. Fix BUG-1 first. A borrow-cost column that reads 0.00% for NVDA/SMCI/BSOL is worse than no column — it actively points users at the most expensive collateral as if it were free. If the vol feed is missing, show “—” or “unavailable,” never 0.00%.
  2. Replace “0% interest” and “0.5% assignment cost” with a per-asset expected-cost table — %/yr of the loan at max LTV, with P(assigned)/week — produced by the same backtest that generates the lender APYs. Wire “Ask Spout’s” “What does this actually cost me?” to return that number.
  3. Pick one assignment model and rewrite the docs around it. If the borrower bears assignment, drop the insurance-fund-covers-assignment language and the “delta-neutral” claim. If the pool bears it, rename the lender product “short-vol equity income” and size the fund to a +10% jump day.
  4. Enroll only the collateral needed to support the loan (e.g. 2× debt) in the call program, leaving the rest un-written. This kills both the “max-out-LTV-or-overpay” incentive and the cross-subsidy in one change.
  5. Offer a cash-settled Auto-Roll that buys back the ITM call at expiry so shares never leave the account — preserving the “no taxable event” promise and the borrower’s leverage.
  6. Reconcile the fee/lockup/KYC pages into one table and delete the contradicting lines; name the auditor with a linked report and the spAsset legal wrapper.
  7. Fix the beta’s testability: ship a one-click in-app devnet faucet, re-verify the Leverage slider with a funded account and real pointer (BUG-2), add balance/max validation to the buy quote (BUG-3), and make the “Borrow” tab an actual borrow-against-holdings flow.

06Methodology & disclosure

What was tested. The live beta at beta.spout.finance on 11 September 2026, on Solana devnet, via a beta access code, driven headless at 1280×800. The wallet connected for real (SIWS message signed); private keys never entered the browser. Console errors across the whole session: zero — the app is stable.

What could not be tested. The wallet held 0 devnet SOL and 0 test USDC, and in-app funding is external-only via two rate-limited/captcha-gated faucets, so I could not broadcast an on-chain buy, borrow or repay. Everything up to the signed transaction — quotes, LTV surface, validation, all read-only UI — was exercised; anything requiring a funded position (health factor, liquidation preview, real mint, partial repay) is marked Blocked above and is the main coverage gap. The Earn/lending product is not live and could not be tested at all.

On the numbers. The economic figures are order-of-magnitude estimates from public documentation and standard option pricing (Black-Scholes, assumed IVs), not audited protocol data. They are meant to size the argument, not to quote Spout’s realised costs. Doc citations (/docs/…) are given so every claim can be checked against the source.
